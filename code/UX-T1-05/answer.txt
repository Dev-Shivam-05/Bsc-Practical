Q: Q5. Explain the History of UNIX.

History of UNIX

- 1965-1969: Bell Labs, MIT and General Electric worked on MULTICS. Bell Labs left the project.
- 1969: Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie at AT&T Bell Labs wrote the first UNIX on a PDP-7 computer, in assembly language.
- 1971: First edition of UNIX released on PDP-11.
- 1973: UNIX was rewritten in the C language (created by Dennis Ritchie). This made UNIX portable to other machines.
- Late 1970s: AT&T gave UNIX source code to universities. University of California, Berkeley developed BSD (Berkeley Software Distribution), adding TCP/IP networking and the vi editor.
- 1983: AT&T released System V. UNIX split into two main families: System V and BSD.
- 1980s-1990s: Commercial versions appeared: SunOS/Solaris (Sun), AIX (IBM), HP-UX (HP), Xenix (Microsoft).
- 1988: POSIX standard defined a common UNIX interface.
- 1991: Linus Torvalds created Linux, a free UNIX-like kernel; with GNU tools it became a complete free OS.
- Today: UNIX ideas live in Linux, macOS (based on BSD/Darwin), Android and most servers.
